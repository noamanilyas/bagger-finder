# Search Seven Portals, Then Go Local

A buyer of a used wheel excavator in Europe gets the widest reach from seven listing databases: Mascus, Machineryline, Truck1, Europe-Construction-Equipment, MachineryZone, TruckScout24 and Maschinensucher. Beyond those, the channels that add different machines are national classifieds (Kleinanzeigen, Otomoto, Landwirt.com, Bazos and FINN B2B stand out), three separate auction groups (Ritchie Bros, Euro Auctions and TBAuctions, whose brands include Troostwijk and Surplex), and German public-sector sales on Zoll-Auktion. Manufacturer portals matter less for finding stock than for warranty: only Liebherr and Volvo CE showed wheel excavators directly, and no official Europe-wide used portal turned up for Hyundai or Yanmar. Headline counts overstate supply, because portals share owners, sellers and even whole databases, and some categories include Chinese stock, toys or auction lots. Costs differ sharply by channel, with auction fees on a EUR 40,000 machine ranging from about EUR 1,300 to possibly EUR 7,600. All counts are one-day snapshots from 7 November 2026 and usually include some new machines. Several major sites blocked automated reading, including Blocket, Willhaben, the Swiss portals and MachineryZone, so a blocked site below means unread, not inactive.

[Machinio](https://www.machinio.com/excavators/wheel-excavators/spain), [Machinery Trader EU](https://machinerytrader.eu/listings/for-sale/atlas/5m), [Exapro](https://www.exapro.com/ps-used-wheeled-excavators/), [TrucksNL](https://trucksnl.com/wheeled-excavators) and [TradeMachines](https://trademachines.com/mobile-excavators) were seen only through search results or returned errors, so they stay secondary until checked by hand. [Tradus](https://www.tradus.com/) looks finished: every URL tried redirected to Otomoto, although no announcement was found and the check ran from what appeared to be a US location. Whether any of these portals charge buyers, or offer saved-search alerts, was not checked.

## National portals reach private sellers, if you use the right section and word

The pan-European portals showed no verified private sellers of wheel excavators, while [Otomoto](https://www.otomoto.pl/maszyny-budowlane/koparki-kolowe) showed 9 private among 32 visible listings. On [Landwirt's small-ads page](https://www.landwirt.com/kleinanzeigen/baumaschinen/mobilbagger), 27 of 31 ads carry the "Kleinanzeige" tag rather than "commercial seller". That tag means less elsewhere: every [TruckScout24](https://www.truckscout24.de/tssp/mobilbagger) card carries it, and the sellers read there were mainly dealers.

**The section matters as much as the site.** [FINN B2B](https://www.finn.no/mobility/search/b2b/construction?q=hjulgraver) returned mostly real machines, while [FINN's consumer section](https://www.finn.no/recommerce/forsale/search?q=hjulgraver) returned mostly parts and toys. [Jófogás Auto](https://auto.jofogas.hu/magyarorszag/jarmu?q=gumikerekes+kotro) returned 31 machines, while [the main Jófogás site](https://www.jofogas.hu/magyarorszag?q=gumikerekes%20kotr%C3%B3) returned none. Searching only the wrong section would suggest a country has no supply. Adding model codes such as PW160, EW140 or A914 should catch ads that omit the type word, although this was inferred from ad titles and not tested.

Check VAT on every ad, because the same market mixes [Otomoto's "netto" tags](https://www.otomoto.pl/maszyny-budowlane/koparki-kolowe), [Autotrader's "+VAT"](https://www.autotrader.co.uk/plant/plant-machinery/excavators/wheeled-excavators) and untagged prices. General classifieds also carry a fraud risk. A [Deutsche Handwerks-Zeitung article](https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/?p=346546), read only through a search summary, describes fake low-priced machine ads that ask for deposits or "escrow" prepayment. No fraud rates were found, but paying nothing before inspection is the sensible rule.

Condition risk sits with the buyer almost everywhere. Troostwijk sells [with "all their visible and invisible defects"](https://www.troostwijkauctions.com/uk/terms), and Klaravik [neither inspects machines nor normally offers viewing during the auction](https://www.klaravik.se/Terms_and_conditions.pdf). Inspect before bidding and book transport before the sale closes, because Klaravik's [complaint window closes when the machine leaves the site](https://www.klaravik.se/Terms_and_conditions.pdf).

TBAuctions [states that lots can appear on its other brands' platforms](https://www.troostwijkauctions.com/uk/terms), so Troostwijk and Surplex are not separate pools. Private buyers have fewer options than the number of auction houses suggests: Troostwijk's terms [provide for consumers](https://www.troostwijkauctions.com/uk/terms) and Auksjonen caters for them, while eligibility at Ritchie Bros, Euro Auctions and Zoll-Auktion was not explicitly confirmed. [BVA Auctions now redirects to Troostwijk](https://www.bva-auctions.com/en). The next sample Ritchie Bros lots [close between 18 November and 3 December](https://www.rbauction.eu/cp/wheel-excavator), although that page shows no year.

## Conclusion

The market looks larger and more varied than it is. Most visible stock comes from commercial sellers reposting the same machines, so another pan-European portal soon stops adding anything new. New supply comes from changing channel or language rather than adding sites: Otomoto, Bazos or Landwirt's small ads reach sellers that Mascus does not show, and Ritchie Bros' French lots or Zoll-Auktion's municipal machines reach different sources again. Buyer type is also a real filter. A private buyer is shut out of Liebherr's portal and Surplex's standard terms, while a trade buyer can use everything but still carries most condition risk at auction. Compare offers on total landed cost, covering VAT treatment, fee, inspection, transport and any warranty, because the list price says little across channels.

Three checks would most improve this picture, all of which need a human browser: reading the blocked portals (MachineryZone, Blocket, Willhaben, Leboncoin, Subito, Milanuncios and the Swiss sites), opening the locators and stock pages for official Hyundai, Develon and Yanmar dealers, and recording an actual premium on a live Troostwijk or Surplex lot. No data was found on how often auction machines turn out defective or on the price gap between auction and dealer, so whether auctions are actually cheaper remains untested here.
